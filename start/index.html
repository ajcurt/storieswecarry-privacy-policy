Getting Started with Stories We Carry

Thank you for trying this out — it means a lot.

Stories We Carry is an app that interviews you about your life. It asks a question out loud, you answer out loud, and it follows up the way a good listener would. Over time it helps you record your stories for your family.

Many of us never tell our stories because we don't know where to begin. This app just asks one question at a time, so you can talk for a few minutes or an hour, whenever it suits you.

You'll only need to set it up once. It takes about 15 minutes. If you'd like, ask someone to sit with you the first time.

You'll get two emails

  1. One from Apple's TestFlight service. It invites you to test Stories We Carry. This is how you install the app. The app isn't in the App Store yet, so this is the only way to get it.
  2. One from Stories We Carry. It has the email address and password you'll use to sign in to the app.

They may arrive at different times. If you can't find one, check your junk or spam folder, or write to us.

Step 1: Install the app

  1. On your iPhone, open the email from TestFlight. (Doing this on a computer or iPad won't work.)
  2. Tap the link in the email. Your iPhone will take you to the App Store to get Apple's free TestFlight app. Install it.
  3. Go back to the email and tap the link again. This time TestFlight opens and shows Stories We Carry. Tap Accept, then Install.
  4. If TestFlight asks to send you notifications, either answer is fine.

When it's done, the Stories We Carry icon is on your Home Screen with your other apps. You won't need TestFlight again except to install updates (see below).

Step 2: Sign in

  1. Open Stories We Carry.
  2. Type the email address and password from the Stories We Carry email. This is not your Apple ID password.
  3. Tap Sign In. You only do this once. The app remembers you after that.

Step 3: Two short questions

Before the first interview, the app reads two short notes out loud. To hear one again, tap Read this aloud again.

  1. "Before we begin." It explains that an AI holds the conversation, and that your stories are never used to train AI. Tap I understand, let's begin.
  2. Keeping your voice. It asks if it may keep recordings of your answers, so your family can one day hear your stories in your own voice. Tap Yes, keep my voice or No, just the words. Either way, everything else works the same.

Step 4: Your first conversation

Before you start: find a quiet spot and turn the volume up. Hold the phone in front of you, or set it on a table close by. Don't hold it to your ear. The questions play from the phone's speaker, not the earpiece.

One setting to change first. In this version, if your screen goes dark while you're talking, the app stops listening and misses your answer. To prevent that, open the iPhone's Settings app, tap Display & Brightness, then Auto-Lock, and choose 5 Minutes (or Never if you like to tell long stories). The next update fixes this, and we'll tell you when you can change it back.

  1. Listen to the question. The first thing it asks about is your name: it checks that it has it right, and asks what you like to be called.
  2. When the question is finished, tap the big Tap to Answer button and start talking. Take as long as you like.
  3. The very first time, your iPhone asks if Stories We Carry may use the microphone. Tap Allow. (If you tapped Don't Allow by mistake: open the iPhone's Settings app, tap Stories We Carry, and turn Microphone on.)
  4. When you've finished, tap Done Talking. The app takes a moment to think, then asks the next question.

Now and then it pauses to ask if you'd like to go on. Tap Keep Going for another question, or Stop For Now.

Stopping, and getting a written copy

  • Stop whenever you like. Tap Stop For Now. It's under the Tap to Answer button. Or tap Tap to Answer and just say "I'd like to stop."
  • Get a written copy. After you stop, tap Email Me This Conversation. The first time, it asks you to confirm; tap Yes, send it to me. The copy goes to the email address you sign in with. The words come from speech recognition, so a few may be misheard.

Next time

Come back whenever you'd like. There's no schedule to keep. It remembers what you've told it and picks up where you left off.

If the app still shows "Thanks for sharing today" when you open it, close it all the way first: swipe up from the bottom of the screen and hold for a moment, then swipe the Stories We Carry picture up and off the screen. Then tap the app icon again. (We're working on making this automatic.)

What to expect

  • It's a conversation, not a form. Talk however you normally would. There's no wrong way to answer.
  • If it gets a name wrong, just tell it. Spell the name out loud ("that's Jon, J-O-N") and it will remember the right spelling.
  • It's fine to pause, or say "I don't remember." It won't push. Memory doesn't work on command, and that's okay.
  • You can go back to anything. If more comes to mind later, just bring it up in another conversation.
  • If it says it didn't hear your answer, the screen probably went dark while you were talking. Change Auto-Lock as described above, then answer again.
  • You need Wi-Fi or a phone signal. If it can't connect, it says so. Tap Try Again.

Updates

From time to time we'll send a new version. TestFlight will let you know. To get it, open the TestFlight app and tap Update next to Stories We Carry. Your stories stay safe through updates.

Tell us what's not working

If something breaks, looks wrong, or confuses you, we want to hear about it. Any of these is fine:

  • Email us at feedback@storieswecarry.app and tell us what happened, in your own words.
  • Or use TestFlight: open the TestFlight app, tap Stories We Carry, then tap Send Beta Feedback. This sends us technical details that help us fix things faster.

Tell us how it felt

After about a week, we'll email you a short survey about the experience itself: whether the pace felt right, whether it asked good questions, and whether you'd want to keep doing it. That's just as valuable to us as bug reports, so please don't skip it.

Your privacy

Your stories are private to your account. Read exactly what we collect and why in our privacy policy.
