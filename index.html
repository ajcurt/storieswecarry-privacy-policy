How it works

  1. You set it up once

    Install the app on their iPhone, sign them in, and say the first hello together. A visit is a good time to do it.

  2. They talk whenever they like

    One answer after breakfast or an hour on a quiet afternoon. They don't need to plan anything or have anyone in the room. "Stop For Now" is always on screen, and saying "I'd like to stop" works too.

  3. Their words come back in writing

    Whenever they choose to email themselves a copy, everything they've said since the last one arrives, with a large-print version ready to print.

A real conversation, kept in their real voice

A conversation, not a questionnaire

There's no list of questions to work through and nothing to fall behind on. It listens to what they say and asks about that: the street, the teacher, the year the family moved. They can steer ("let's talk about my mission," "ask me how I met my husband"), and next time it remembers where they left off. If they say "I don't remember," it moves on kindly. If they say "please don't ask me about that," it won't bring it up again.

Their real voice, kept, not copied

With their permission, it keeps the recording of every answer: their laugh, the pauses, the way they say a name. If they'd rather not be recorded, they can say no and everything else works the same. We don't make computer copies of anyone's voice, and we never put words in their mouth.

Made for listening, not reading

Every question is spoken in a natural voice, and they answer by talking. Even the permission screens read themselves aloud. We tested it with VoiceOver, the screen reader built into every iPhone. It works for parents who can't see the screen well, and for anyone who would simply rather talk than write.

“What would you give to be able to hear your grandmother's grandmother's voice describing her life in her own words? At the same time, what is it worth to you to make sure that your granddaughter's granddaughter can hear your voice describing your life in your own words?”

AJ Curtis, Founder

Their own voice, kept for the family

An answer can only be recorded once, in the moment it's spoken, so we built recording first. Ways for the family to listen come next.

Recordings are encrypted, kept for the long term, and never used to train AI.

What comes out of it

A catch-up email in their own words

Everything they've said since the last email, oldest first, each sitting under its date. Skip a week and nothing is lost; the next email catches up. It goes to their own email address, and they can forward it to whoever they like.

A large-print copy, ready to print

Each email carries a PDF in big, clear type (Atkinson Hyperlegible, a typeface designed for people with low vision), with their name at the top and page numbers. Names with accents and the ʻokina print correctly. Print it at home and hand it around at Sunday dinner.

Their voice, saved

The recording of every answer, kept for the long term, with their permission.

Planned: FamilySearch Memories

Their stories stay theirs

  • Their stories belong to them. They stay private unless they choose to share them with family.
  • Never sold, never shared with advertisers, and never used to train AI.
  • Recordings are encrypted and kept separately for each person.
  • They can ask us to delete any answer, a conversation, or everything. Recordings are permanently deleted within 7 days.
  • Anything they ask it never to bring up stays private. Family never sees that list.
  • Before the first conversation, it explains out loud that it's an AI and asks their permission. Ask it how it works and it will tell you.
  • They're in charge: they can say no to recording, stop any time, and change the subject whenever they like.

Read the privacy policy

Will my mom or dad actually use it?

We built it around that question. There's nothing to read, type or keep up with, and nothing nags them: no streaks, no “you haven't talked in a while.” They talk when they feel like it, for as long or short as they like. No one can promise every parent will take to it. If yours doesn't, write to us and we'll help.

Will I end up running it?

You help with setup once. After that there are no questions to choose, no calls to schedule and no chapters to edit. The written copy goes to them by email whenever they ask for it.

Is it a subscription?

Not during the beta: it's free, and pricing isn't set yet. When paid plans start, the life-story gift will be one payment for a year of conversations. It won't auto-renew. We'll remind you before the year ends, and everything they've recorded stays theirs.

Can both of our parents use it?

Yes. Each parent gets their own account, so their stories stay separate. During the beta it's free for both.

What do they need?

An iPhone with iOS 17 or later, Wi-Fi or cellular data, and a quiet spot. It also works on iPad; we use one every day while building it. It speaks English for now. It doesn't work on a landline or a flip phone yet. Someone may need to help with the first setup and sign-in.

Who gets the written copy?

It's emailed to your parent, at the address on their own account. From there they can share it however they like.

Who do I talk to if something goes wrong?

Write to hello@storieswecarry.app. The person who built it reads every note and writes back.

Your stories count too

We built Stories We Carry first for parents and grandparents. But anyone with stories worth keeping can use it, at any age from 18 up. If you'd like to try it yourself, say so when you write.

Why I built this

A lot of people mean to tell their life story and never start. They don't know where to begin, they edit themselves before they've said anything, and it never feels like there's time. I built Stories We Carry so the asking is taken care of, in short bursts or long sessions, without putting anyone out.

If it isn't working for your mom or dad, I want to know. Write to me and I'll answer myself.

For me, it's part of turning the hearts of the children to their fathers, and the hearts of the fathers to their children.

AJ Curtis, Founder

Why "Stories We Carry"

Every family carries stories: the ones our parents told us, and the ones only they can tell. We carry them with us, and one day we hand them to the people who come after us. The name is a reminder that those stories are ours to keep and to pass on, and that they're worth recording before they're lost.
